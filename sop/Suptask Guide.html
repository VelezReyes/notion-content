Overview
What Suptask is and how it fits into daily work at VélezReyes+.
What is Suptask?

Suptask is our internal ticketing system, built directly into Slack. It lets anyone on the team create structured requests (for HR, legal, design, operations, or anything that requires a tracked response) without leaving the tools we already use every day.

Every ticket goes to the right team through the right channel, with the right context attached. No lost messages, no unclear ownership.

Two ways to open a ticket
Method 1
/suptask

Type /suptask in any Slack chat (a DM or a channel). A form selector will appear. Pick the right form and fill it out.

Works everywhere in Slack.

Method 2
🎫 Ticket Emoji Reaction

React to any existing Slack message with the ticket emoji. Suptask converts that message into a ticket automatically.

The message becomes the ticket context.

Which method should I use?
We recommend the emoji reaction 🎫 for most cases: it's the easiest and fastest way. Share your context in Slack as you normally would, then react to your message with the ticket emoji to turn it into a tracked request. Suptask captures the message and its thread automatically. Use /suptask when you need to start a ticket from scratch without an existing message.

Priority Levels
Four levels. Use them consistently so the team can manage the queue effectively.
Working days only
All response and resolution times below are measured in working days (weekdays only). Public holidays and non-working days do not count toward these timelines. If a deadline falls on a holiday, it moves to the next working day.
Priority Resolution Window When to use it
Critical 1 working day Major incident, immediate legal or financial risk, any matter that requires or directly involves an executive-level decision, or anything directly affecting D+M.
High Up to 4 working days Significant impact, but not fully blocked. Needs attention without delay.
Medium Up to 7 working days Important but can wait for the normal queue. No immediate blockers.
Low No fixed date · Best effort No urgency. Informational, optional, or nice to have addressed eventually.
⚠️ Due Diligence & Legal: External dependency
For Due Diligence and Legal tickets, internal response times above still apply; the team will acknowledge and engage quickly. However, resolution depends on external parties (trust and/or lawyers) whose availability is outside VélezReyes+' control.

When opening these tickets: include any external deadline in the ticket itself, set expectations with your stakeholders upfront, and do not treat the internal SLA as a commitment for final resolution.
Best Practices
Things that make tickets faster to resolve and easier to manage. Tap each to read more.
Always include complete context in your ticket ▾

The person receiving your ticket may have no background on the situation. Include: what the request is, why it matters, who it affects, and any relevant deadline.

A ticket that needs follow-up questions before it can be acted on creates unnecessary delays. Write it as if the reader knows nothing about the context.

Use the right channel for channel-based forms ▾

Channel forms (Design/Comms, Due Diligence, Legal, Sign Documents) only appear in their designated channels. If you try to open them from the wrong channel, the form won't show up.

Before opening a ticket, confirm you're in the right place. If you're unsure, ask your team lead.

Keep private forms in DMs, not public channels ▾

Forms like HR, Deel, and Profit.co contain personal or sensitive information. Always open them from a direct message, never from a public or team channel.

This protects your privacy and ensures the request reaches the right person without exposing it to the broader team.

Set the priority honestly ▾

Prioritize based on actual impact and urgency, not on how quickly you personally want something done. Overusing Critical or High makes those levels meaningless and forces the team to re-triage the entire queue.

If you're unsure, default to Medium. The team can always escalate once they see the full context of the ticket.

For Legal and Due Diligence: set expectations early ▾

These tickets involve external parties (trust, lawyers) whose timelines are not under internal control. The moment you open the ticket, communicate to your stakeholders that resolution will depend on external availability, not just internal SLAs.

Include any hard external deadline in the ticket itself. Monitor actively and follow up proactively if there's no movement.

The golden rule
A good ticket is one the recipient can act on immediately, without asking a single follow-up question. The more complete and clear your ticket, the faster it gets resolved.
Ticket Statuses
Six statuses. They tell you exactly where a ticket stands at any point in time. Tap each to read more.
Open ▾

The ticket has been submitted and is sitting in the queue. It has not been picked up yet. No action needed from the requester at this point.

In Progress ▾

Someone on the team has picked up the ticket and is actively working on it. You may receive follow-up questions or updates through the ticket thread in Slack.

Escalated ▾

The ticket has been escalated to an external party, typically the trust and/or external lawyers. Internal handling is complete for now, and resolution depends on the availability and response of those external parties.

This status is most common for Legal and Due Diligence tickets. Resolution timelines are outside internal control once escalated.

Waiting for Customer ▾

The ticket is on hold waiting for a response or action from the person who submitted it, or from a third party involved in the request.

This applies when:

The team is waiting on the requester to provide additional information, a missing document, or a decision that is needed to move forward.

It also applies when the team is waiting on an external person involved in the request, such as a beneficiary, an organization, or an initiative, to submit or send a document.

Action needed from you
If your ticket shows this status, check the thread in Slack. There will be a message explaining what is needed. The sooner you respond, the sooner the ticket can move forward.
On Hold ▾

The ticket has been temporarily paused. This usually happens when work cannot continue until an external decision is made, an internal alignment is reached, or a dependency is resolved.

On Hold is different from Waiting for Customer: the blocker here is not necessarily on the requester's side. The team will update the ticket when it is ready to move again.

Closed ▾

The request has been resolved and the ticket is complete. No further action is expected. If the issue resurfaces or something was missed, open a new ticket with a reference to the original one.

Stay on top of your tickets: digest notifications

Suptask can send you a periodic summary of all your open tickets directly in Slack, so you never lose track of what is pending.

How to set up ticket summary notifications
Open the Suptask App in your Slack sidebar, go to your notification settings, and enable the digest summary. You can choose the frequency that works best for you.
