Suptask Ticketing Guide

How Administration & Operations requests are submitted, tracked, and resolved across the organization.

Core Principle
Slack messages are for communication. Suptask tickets are for execution and follow-up.
If something needs tracking, ownership, prioritization, or follow-up — it should be a Suptask ticket.
Overview
What Suptask is, when to use it, and what falls outside its scope.

Suptask is the internal ticketing system embedded inside Slack. It manages operational requests, cross-functional work, and anything that requires tracking, ownership, or follow-up. It applies to all team members at VélezReyes+ and Vere, regardless of role or country.

A direct message saying "Can you help me with this next week?" is not a reliable commitment — a ticket is.

When to create a ticket
Operational Request

Any action needed from another person or team — Operations, HR, Deel, Design, Comms, Due Diligence, Legal, Travel, Sign Documents, or any category not listed.

Could Get Lost in Chat

If forgetting a request would create friction, delays, or risk, it needs a ticket — not a message that scrolls away.

Multi-Step or Cross-Team

When multiple people or teams are involved, Suptask creates shared visibility. Example: new hire onboarding touches HR, IT, Operations, and Finance — all in one ticket.

Needs Documentation

Approvals, recurring processes, vendor conversations, operational changes, and legal or financial requests all require a written record.

When NOT to create a ticket
Out of scope
Casual conversations  ·  Brainstorming  ·  Quick questions with immediate answers  ·  FYIs requiring no action  ·  Decisions resolved in under a few minutes.
Rule of thumb
"Are you joining the call?" → no ticket.   "Can you book my travel for next week?" → create a ticket.
Installation
Steps to install Suptask in your Slack workspace and confirm admin access before configuration.
Admin role required
Installing Suptask requires a Slack Workspace Admin role. If you are not an admin, you can ask your Slack Admin to install it using the steps below, request the Admin role temporarily, or submit an app install request — an Admin will receive a notification to approve it.
1
Install Suptask Start here
Log in with Slack and grant the required permissions
▼
  • 1
    Open app.suptask.com and log in with your Slack account.
    Make sure you are logging in with the correct Slack workspace — you can toggle this in the upper right corner.
  • 2
    Review and grant the permissions Suptask requires to be installed.
    You may be asked to confirm permissions twice — once for your user and once for the workspace.
  • 3
    The installation wizard will confirm when Suptask is successfully installed.
    Verify by searching for Suptask in your Slack workspace.
2
Approve Suptask for the Workspace
Required if your Slack workspace enforces app approvals
▼
  • 1
    Go to the Slack Admin interface and navigate to the Suptask App page in the Slack Marketplace.
  • 2
    Confirm the app status is set to Approved for the Workspace.
  • 3
    If it is not approved, approve it from the Admin interface. After this, Suptask will be available to all workspace members.
Next step
With Suptask installed and admin access confirmed, proceed to set up your first Inbox and Form — then your team is ready to start submitting tickets.

Create a Ticket
Pick the method that fits your context — then fill the form correctly.
Before your first ticket
Confirm Suptask is installed in the VélezReyes+ Slack workspace (search "Suptask" in Slack). For DM tickets, grant extended permissions once at app.suptask.com/public/auth-dm.
1
From Any Slack Message Recommended
Convert any existing message directly into a ticket
▼
  • 1
    Hover over the message you want to convert into a ticket.
  • 2
    Click the three-dot menu (More actions) that appears to the right of the message.
  • 3
    Select Create a ticket (Suptask). If not visible, choose More message shortcuts… to find it.
  • 4
    Fill in the ticket form using the field guide below and submit.
2
Ticket Emoji Reaction 🎫
Fastest method for channel messages
▼
  • 1
    Hover over any message and click Add reaction.
  • 2
    Search and select the ticket emoji :ticket: 🎫.
  • 3
    Suptask opens the ticket creation form automatically.
  • 4
    If it's your first time in that channel, type /invite @Suptask in the channel first, then retry.
3
Via Slash Command
Create from scratch in any channel or DM
▼
  • 1
    From any channel or DM, type one of these commands and press Enter:
    /suptask   /request   /helpdesk   /support   /ticket
  • 2
    Select the form that matches your request type.
  • 3
    Fill in all required fields using the guide below and submit.
4
From Suptask App Home
Central view for creating and reviewing your open tickets
▼
  • 1
    Open the Suptask App in Slack from the left-side menu. Search "Suptask" if it's not pinned.
  • 2
    Click Create a new ticket.
  • 3
    Select the inbox (team) and form type, fill in the fields below, and submit.
5
In a Direct Message (DM)
Create tickets directly from private conversations
▼
  • 1
    Use any of the methods above (message shortcut, emoji reaction, or slash command) from within a Direct Message.
  • 2
    If this is your first time using the 🎫 emoji reaction in a DM, you may need to grant Suptask extended permissions.
    Grant them once at app.suptask.com/public/auth-dm — this is a one-time step per user.
  • 3
    Fill in the ticket form and submit. Done.
6
From Email
Send an email that automatically becomes a ticket in Slack
▼
  • 1
    Send an email to the address configured for the Email-to-Slack integration in your workspace.
    Ask the Suptask Admin for the correct inbox email address.
  • 2
    Suptask converts the email into a ticket automatically. The email body is populated in the Description field.
  • 3
    The ticket thread appears inside Slack and can be managed like any other ticket.
7
Automatic Ticket Creation from a Channel
Every new message in a channel becomes a ticket automatically
▼
  • 1
    This method is enabled by the Suptask Admin per channel — no setup needed from the submitter.
  • 2
    Post a message in a channel where automatic ticket creation is enabled.
  • 3
    Suptask automatically creates the ticket. Your message content is placed in the Description field.
Admin only
Automatic ticket creation must be configured per channel by the Suptask Admin. Contact the Director of People & Operations to request it for a specific channel.

How to fill the form

Once the ticket form is open — regardless of which method you used — fill in every field as follows. Incomplete tickets get delayed or routed incorrectly.

Field What to enter Tips
Submit to Select Inbox (team inbox) Use Inbox for most requests. Channel is for shared-channel visibility.
Who do you need help from? Select the team: HR, Operations, Legal, etc. This routes the ticket to the right inbox. Wrong assignment causes delays.
What type of request? Select the request category Matches the team selected and defines which form fields appear.
Who is requesting? Your name — auto-filled Confirm it reflects your correct Slack profile.
Title Clear, specific, action-oriented Good: "Review Deel contract for new hire — deadline June 5"
Avoid: "Help" · "Quick question" · "Review this"
Description Full context: background, deadline, links, docs, stakeholders Assume the reader has zero prior context. The ticket must be self-contained.
Tag Select one tag from the taxonomy below Required field. See the tag list below to pick the right one.
Priority High / Medium / Low Be honest — not everything is High. See Lifecycle & Priority tab for definitions.
Tag Taxonomy — select exactly one

Every ticket requires one tag. Pick the one that best describes what you need.

Tag Use when…
Operations Process coordination, logistics, vendor setup, event support
Human Resources HR inquiries, onboarding, offboarding, benefits, internal HR platform
Deel Contractor contracts, payments, Deel platform actions
Design Creative asset requests, brand deliverables, visual reviews
Comms Announcement drafts, content reviews, publishing coordination
Due Diligence Partner or vendor reviews, background checks
Legal Contract reviews, compliance requests, legal opinions
Sign Documents Signature coordination for any document type
Travel Flight and hotel booking, travel approvals, logistics
General Question Questions that don't fit another category but need a tracked answer
Others Any operational request not covered by the tags above

Lifecycle & Priority
How every ticket moves from Open to Closed — and what each priority level means.
Ticket Lifecycle
1
Open
Owner
Review and acknowledge the ticket. Confirm ownership or reassign.
Requester: confirm the ticket has enough context. Add anything missing.
2
In Progress
Owner
Update progress in the ticket thread. Flag blockers early — do not wait until the deadline.
Requester: respond promptly to follow-up questions.
3
Blocked
Owner
Communicate the blocker clearly in the ticket thread. Do not leave it in silence.
Requester: help resolve the blocker where possible.
4
Resolved
Owner
Mark the ticket as resolved. Add a brief summary of what was done.
Requester: confirm the request was fulfilled. Reopen if something is missing.
5
Closed
No further action required from either side.
Priority Definitions
Priority Definition Examples
High Urgent blockers or time-sensitive operational risks. Legal deadline today · Flight booking in 24h · System access blocking work
Medium Important but not blocking — should be resolved this week. Contract review due in 3 days · Reimbursement approval · Onboarding prep
Low Nice-to-have or long-term with no immediate deadline. Policy drafts · Recurring vendor renewals · Non-urgent IT requests
Exceptions
Urgent requests that cannot wait for formal ticket creation should be communicated in Slack first, then converted into a Suptask ticket immediately after. The ticket is the official record even when the conversation started informally. Exceptions require approval from the Director of People & Operations and must be documented in the ticket thread.
Roles & Best Practices
Who is responsible for what — and how to use Suptask well.
Roles
Role
Requester
  • Create clear, specific tickets — not vague or incomplete.
  • Add all context upfront: background, deadlines, links, stakeholders.
  • Define urgency honestly — not everything is High priority.
  • Respond promptly to follow-up questions from the owner.
Role
Owner / Agent
  • Acknowledge the ticket when received.
  • Communicate blockers early — not at the deadline.
  • Keep the ticket status updated throughout.
  • Close the ticket once the request is fully resolved.
Role
Suptask Admin
  • Configures inboxes, forms, and permissions.
  • Requires Slack Workspace Admin role at setup.
  • Manages user access at app.suptask.com → Users & Permissions.
Role
Director of P&O
  • Primary contact and policy owner.
  • Escalation point for policy questions.
  • Approves exceptions to the SOP.
  • Conducts the annual policy review.
Best Practices
💬
Keep communication inside the ticket
Reply in the ticket thread, not in a separate DM or channel message. This keeps context centralized and visible to anyone who needs it.
🔍
Avoid duplicate tickets
Before creating a ticket, do a quick search in Suptask to check whether one already exists for the same request.
📝
Use tickets instead of mental notes
If you think "I will remember this later" — create a ticket instead. Memory is not a reliable operational system.
✏️
Write titles that work on their own
Good: "Review Deel contract for new hire — deadline June 5" · "Coordinate June São Paulo travel — 4 people" · "Approve Q2 conference reimbursement"
Avoid: "Help" · "Quick question" · "Travel" · "Review this"
Troubleshooting
Common issues and how to resolve them quickly.
Issue Solution
Ticket emoji does nothing in a channel Invite Suptask to the channel first: type /invite @Suptask
Cannot use Suptask in a DM Grant extended DM permissions once at app.suptask.com/public/auth-dm
"Create a ticket" not visible in message menu Select More message shortcuts… to find it in the full list.
Ticket sent to the wrong team Reopen the ticket and update the inbox/assignee, or create a new one with correct routing.
Suptask not found in Slack Ask the Suptask Admin to confirm the app is installed and approved for the workspace.
No Admin access in Suptask after install Log in to app.suptask.com and ask an existing Admin to promote your account in Users & Permissions.
