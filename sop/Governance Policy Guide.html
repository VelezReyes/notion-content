Governance Policy Guide

How organizational policies are created, approved, published, and maintained. · Effective 2026

Organizational policies are the shared rules that define how VélezReyes+ and Vere work together. They apply to everyone on the team, regardless of role or country, and are reviewed regularly to stay current.

If you need to create or review a policy, the Policy Lifecycle and Checklist tabs walk you through the full process. If you are looking for an existing policy, go to the Policy Directory.

Overview
What organizational policies are, what to expect from them, and how they stay current.
What is an organizational policy?

An organizational policy is a shared rule that applies to every team member at VélezReyes+ and Vere, regardless of role, seniority, or country. Policies exist to create consistency, protect everyone's rights, and make sure the company operates within legal and ethical boundaries.

Policies are different from team-level SOPs or project playbooks. A policy sets a company-wide standard. An SOP explains how to execute it in a specific context. Both matter, but one without the other is incomplete.

What counts as an organizational policy?
Any rule that applies to all team members across VélezReyes+ and Vere, including HR and talent policies. Team-specific SOPs, project-level procedures, client playbooks, and role-specific guidelines are out of scope and do not go through this process.

Review cadence

Every organizational policy is reviewed at least once per year. If a review leads to material changes, both CEOs must approve the revision before it is republished and the team is notified.

Reviews can also be triggered at any time by a relevant change: a new regulation, an organizational restructure, a new country or tool, or an incident that reveals a gap in the current policy.

Retroactive or incomplete policies
Any policy that was published or enforced without completing all seven steps must be submitted retroactively for CEO approval or formally retired. There are no informal workarounds.
Retiring a policy

When a policy is superseded or no longer needed, move the files to Google Drive /Policies/Retired/ with the supersession date noted. Update the Notion Employee Hub entry to point to the current version. The retired document is kept for reference but is no longer active, and the team is notified.

Exceptions and deviations

Exceptions to the governance process itself (missing steps, incomplete deliverables) must be resolved by submitting the policy retroactively for CEO approval or formally retiring it. There are no informal workarounds.

Exceptions to the terms of a specific policy are handled inside that policy's own text. Contact the Director of People & Operations if you believe an exception applies to your situation.

Common questions
What is the difference between a policy and an SOP? ▾
A policy sets the rule: what applies, to whom, and why. A Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) explains how to execute the rule in practice, step by step. Most policies have a corresponding SOP. Both are published together and linked from the Notion Employee Hub.
Can a manager create a team-level rule without going through this process? ▾
Yes, within their team's scope. This governance process applies only to transversal organizational policies that affect all team members. Team-specific SOPs, project-level procedures, and role-specific guidelines are out of scope and do not require CEO approval through this process. Managers cannot create rules that contradict a published policy.
How do I know if a policy change is "material" and requires re-approval? ▾
Any change that alters entitlements, obligations, or procedures for team members is material and requires going back through the full approval cycle. Corrections of typos, formatting updates, or link replacements are not material. When in doubt, consult the Director of People & Operations before publishing.
What if we need a policy urgently and cannot wait for the full lifecycle? ▾
Urgency does not remove the requirement for both CEO sign-offs. What can be expedited is the drafting and review timeline. People & Operations can run a compressed review cycle with the CEOs. A policy communicated before both approvals are obtained must be framed as a draft or interim guidance, not as a published policy.
Can I suggest a new policy or flag that something is missing? ▾
Yes. Any team member can raise the need for a new organizational policy. Reach out to the Director of People & Operations with the context: what gap you are seeing, who it affects, and why a company-wide standard would help. People & Operations will evaluate it and, if appropriate, initiate the lifecycle process.
